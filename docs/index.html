    Getting started

    1. Open a recording from the SD card on the Home page (or drop the file on the page), connect the sensor on the Device page, or pick an example.
    2. The Analysis page has three views. Overview: the shape of the stroke and its numbers. 3D: the oar or the boat and sculler moving. Over time: the whole session as charts.
    3. The player at the bottom plays the session back. Click or drag on any chart to jump; Space plays and pauses; on the stroke profile, ← → step 1 % of the stroke.

    Oar sensor

    Fix the sensor to the shaft, inboard or outboard of the pin; tell the viewer which in Settings. It works out which way the sensor points from the rowing itself, once there are a few strokes.

    Zero it: hold the oar perpendicular to the boat, tap the gunwale 3 times and hold still until the LED lights solid. Sweep angles are then true angles from perpendicular, from that moment until the next zero (and back to the start of the file for the first). Without a zero, sweep is relative to the average oar position.

    A 6-axis sensor has no compass, so slow heading drift and turns of the boat are removed with the drift window in Settings. The gyro's bias is re-measured whenever the oar is still for a second, so hold it still for a few seconds after switching on.

    Catch and finish are told apart automatically: the drive is the half of the stroke where the blade is lower.

    Boat sensor

    Fix the sensor flat to the hull or a rigger, anywhere near the middle of the boat. The fore-aft axis is found from the data: it is the direction the boat accelerates in, and forward is the way it accelerates for most of the stroke. Set it by hand in Settings if detection gets it wrong.

    The catch is marked at the deepest dip in acceleration around the front of the stroke (Kleshnev's point 6, the negative peak). For good technique that is within about 50 ms of the moment the oar reverses; with a long, shallow check it can land on either of two dips. The finish is marked at the release dip. A sensor on an oar gives the exact catch.

    The numbers

    Each tile shows the current stroke in large type and the session average under it.

    Rate
    Strokes per minute.
    Arc
    Total oar angle from catch to finish.
    Catch / finish angle
    Oar angle from perpendicular at the catch and at the finish (true angles only after a zero).
    Drive : recovery
    Time with the blade in the water against time out of it. Below 1: a longer recovery, usual at low rates.
    Check
    The deepest deceleration around the catch, in m/s². Smaller is better: the rower's mass reversing and the blade not yet holding.
    Drive peak
    The highest acceleration during the drive.
    Time to peak
    From the catch to the drive peak. Short for a front-loaded drive, longer when legs and trunk work together.
    Speed change
    How much the boat's speed swings within the stroke (max − min). Smaller wastes less energy.
    Set (roll σ)
    How much the boat rolls from side to side during the stroke, in degrees. Smaller is a steadier boat.

    What is measured, and what is modelled

    • Measured: the oar's sweep, height and feather (oar sensor); the boat's fore-aft acceleration, pitch and roll (boat sensor). Speed change within the stroke comes from integrating the acceleration.
    • Assumed: the boat's average speed. Without GPS the sensor only knows how the speed changes, so absolute movement uses the speed you set (or the model's speed for the examples).
    • Modelled: the sculler in the 3D boat view. For the examples it replays the exact simulated technique; for your recordings it is a standard legs-trunk-arms sequence timed to the detected catch and finish, not your actual body movement.
    • Animation only: the catch delay and the exaggeration. They change the picture, not the data or the numbers.

    Files

    Copy ROWxxxx.CSV from the SD card and open it. Columns t_ms, ax_mg, ay_mg, az_mg, gx_ddps, gy_ddps, gz_ddps; tap-zero moments are stored as #CAL lines.

    Other CSVs load too: columns t and ax…gz in g and °/s, or stroke-window logs from the older boat logger (StrokeID, Phase, t_ms, ax, ay, az). Those only hold a window around each stroke; the pauses between windows are left out of the numbers.

    Live connection

    Bluetooth works in Chrome or Edge on Android, Windows, macOS and ChromeOS; USB needs Chrome or Edge on a computer. Safari and iOS have neither. If the connect buttons are blocked, the page is embedded somewhere: open it directly.

    While connected, the sensor's mode (oar or boat), SD logging and speaker are controlled from the Device page. When you disconnect, the live session stays loaded and can be played back.
